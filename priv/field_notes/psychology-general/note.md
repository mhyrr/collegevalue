Psychology graduates of public universities and private nonprofit colleges earn almost exactly the same in their first year. The private school typically costs a middle-income family about $86,000 over four years, against about $56,000 at a public university. Graduates of for-profit schools earn a little less, and their typical debt takes 24.4 months of pay to repay, compared with 9.9 months at public schools.

For many students, the bachelor's degree is the first step: the American Psychological Association reports that close to half of psychology bachelor's holders go on to earn a graduate degree. Becoming a psychologist takes a doctorate, and counseling roles require a master's, so first-year pay shows only the start of that path. Any debt from the first degree comes along to the next one, which makes a low-cost bachelor's worth looking for.
