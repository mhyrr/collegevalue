Computer and Information Sciences graduates of the nine schools that admit under a tenth of applicants earn about $102,000 in their first year, nearly twice the about $54,000 at the 272 schools that admit most applicants. Career offices at those top schools list Google, Microsoft, Amazon and JPMorgan Chase among their graduates' main employers, and those seats are few. A typical graduate also goes straight to work, since software jobs need only the bachelor's, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

At an ordinary school, what you pay sets the deal more than where you go. For-profit programs pay about what public ones do but leave graduates owing about $51,000, or 10.2 months of first-year pay against 5.2 at public schools. Two programs 7 miles apart in the Miami area pay within a few thousand dollars of each other, yet one costs a middle-income family about $94,000 over four years and the other about $35,000; pick the lower cost and borrow as little as you can.
