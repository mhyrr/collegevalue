Finance splits into two job markets. Graduates of schools that admit most applicants typically earn about $48,000 in their first year, while the few programs at schools admitting under a tenth pay about $86,000. One highly selective school's career office reported that almost half of a recent class went into investment banking or consulting, the best-paid entry jobs in the field, and typical finance programs barely reach that market (The Daily Pennsylvanian, 2015).

For a family earning $48,000 to $75,000, a public finance program typically costs about $57,000 over four years. A private nonprofit costs about $92,000, and its graduates earn only a little more. For-profit programs leave the heaviest debt, about 7.7 months of first-year pay against 5.7 at public schools.
