CUNY Hunter College and Montclair State University, two public History programs 14 miles apart, pay their graduates about the same. At Hunter, the typical loan takes 5.0 months of first-year pay to repay. At Montclair State it takes 11.6 months, and both admit students straight from high school.

The American Historical Association, using Census survey data, reports that more than half of History graduates go on to graduate or professional school, including law school. First-year pay understates where many of them end up, and that next degree usually brings its own debt. A History degree that costs less leaves more room for the one after it.
