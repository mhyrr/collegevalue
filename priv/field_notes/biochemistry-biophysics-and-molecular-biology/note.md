Biochemistry graduates earn about the same in their first year whether their school admits under a tenth of applicants or most of them. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says research biochemist jobs usually require a doctoral degree, so a bachelor's leads to entry-level lab work that pays alike from any campus. Many graduates are still in medical or graduate school, so first-year pay understates where the major leads.

Because the school's name barely moves pay, let the price drive your choice. A middle-income family typically pays about $61,000 over four years at schools that admit most applicants and about $42,000 at the most selective ones. Occidental College and the University of Southern California, 8 miles apart in Los Angeles, pay about the same, yet Occidental leaves graduates owing 14.1 months of pay and USC 1.8, so compare net prices and borrow as little as you can before the extra schooling a research career requires.
