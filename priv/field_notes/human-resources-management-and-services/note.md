Only two programs sit among the schools that admit under a tenth of applicants, and their graduates mostly leave HR. At the larger one, Cornell's ILR School, more graduates go into financial services and management consulting at firms like McKinsey and JPMorgan than into corporate HR jobs, according to the school's career outcomes report. Below that tier, pay is nearly flat: graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn about what graduates of moderately selective schools earn.

Since the school's name barely moves pay, the price does. A family earning $48,000 to $75,000 typically pays about $58,000 over four years at a public university and about $87,000 at a private nonprofit, for nearly the same first-year pay. For-profit graduates owe 10.5 months of pay against 6.7 at public schools, so start with your in-state public program and borrow as little as you can.
