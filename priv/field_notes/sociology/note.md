Paying for a more selective school buys Sociology graduates nothing unless the school admits under a tenth of applicants. Schools that admit 10 to 60 percent pay about what open schools pay. Only the few most selective schools send graduates into the finance, consulting and tech jobs that recruit there across every major.

Public and private nonprofit programs pay about the same, but a middle-income family typically pays about $84,000 over four years at a private school against about $57,000 at a public one. In the American Sociological Association's national survey, most graduates went straight to work, often in social services, counseling or clerical jobs, and only about a tenth were in graduate school. Expect a first job like those, and weigh the cost of the school against it.
