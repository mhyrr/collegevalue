Electrical Engineering graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn about $69,000 in their first year, close to the about $79,000 earned at schools that admit under a tenth. Employers hire new electrical engineers with a bachelor's from an accredited program and no license, so most graduates go straight into engineering jobs, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

The school's name moves pay so little that the price decides the deal. At two programs 5 miles apart in Orange County, California, graduates earn about the same, yet one leaves them owing about $36,000, or 6.8 months of pay, against about $15,000 and 2.7 months at the other, and its four years cost a middle-income family about $120,000 against about $22,000. Choose an accredited program at the lowest cost you can find, and check your nearest public campus first.
