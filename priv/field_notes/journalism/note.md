Two Journalism programs 5 miles apart in the San Diego area pay their graduates about the same: about $34,000 at the public campus and about $30,000 at the for-profit online program. The for-profit leaves its graduates owing about $43,000, which takes 17.0 months of pay to repay, against about $15,000 and 5.3 months at the public school. A middle-income family also pays about $106,000 there over four years, against about $57,000.

Outside the few most selective schools, the school barely moves Journalism pay, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics projects reporting jobs to shrink over the coming decade. Private nonprofits pay about $33,000 against about $31,000 at publics, yet they cost a middle-income family about $100,000 over four years against about $59,000. Pick the lowest-cost program that has a working campus paper or station, since BLS says that experience and internships help new reporters get hired.
