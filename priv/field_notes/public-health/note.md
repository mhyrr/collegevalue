Public Health pay barely depends on the school: graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn about what graduates of schools admitting a tenth to a quarter earn. A University of Minnesota study of recent graduates found most start in entry jobs at private companies and in health care, with no employer paying extra for a selective degree. The price varies far more: for-profit programs leave graduates owing 19.7 months of pay, against 8.3 at public universities, and they pay less.

A national study in the American Journal of Public Health found more than three quarters of graduates were working soon after finishing, and only about one in eight went straight on to more school, so first-year pay is close to what you will actually start at. The highest figures come from Utah State and Illinois State, whose numbers likely include accredited industrial hygiene or environmental health tracks that train workplace-hazard specialists for better-paid jobs in private industry. Choose the lowest-cost public program you can, and look for one of those tracks if you want more than a typical entry salary.
