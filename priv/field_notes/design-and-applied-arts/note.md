In Design and Applied Arts, the price of a school varies far more than what its graduates earn. Two programs 9 miles apart in the San Diego area pay about the same, yet the for-profit one leaves its typical graduate with a loan worth 15.4 months of first-year pay, against 4.8 at the public university. Nationally, a middle-income family typically pays about $127,000 over four years at a for-profit design school and about $56,000 at a public university, and the for-profit graduates earn less.

Design graduates go straight to work with a bachelor's degree and a portfolio, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics projects graphic design jobs, the most common path, to shrink slightly. The better-paid product and industrial design work has few openings a year, so the price you pay matters more than the name on the degree. Build your portfolio at a public university, where a middle-income family typically pays far less than at private or for-profit art schools.
