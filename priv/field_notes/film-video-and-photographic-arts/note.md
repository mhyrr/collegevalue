Two film programs 10 miles apart in the San Francisco Bay Area pay their graduates about the same in the first year, yet one leaves them owing 13.8 months of pay and the other 6.0. That gap runs through the whole field. For-profit film schools take a median 18.8 months of pay to repay against 11.7 at public universities, cost a middle-income family about $127,000 over four years against about $62,000, and still pay their graduates less.

Most film and camera work is freelance and project-based, with no license or pay scale, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics finds that many editors and camera operators are self-employed and move from job to job. A school's name lifts first-year pay only a little, so your reel and your debt will matter more than where you went. If you want to work in Film and Photography, start with the public universities in your state that teach production, and choose the one that costs your family least.
