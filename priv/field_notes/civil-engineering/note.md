In Civil Engineering, the school's selectivity barely changes first-year pay. Graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn about $61,000, and graduates of schools that admit under a tenth earn about $68,000. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says engineers move up through licensing: most take the FE exam near graduation, then work about four years under a licensed engineer before they can earn the PE license that lets them sign plans.

Price varies far more than pay. Private nonprofit schools typically cost a middle-income family about $97,000 over four years, against about $57,000 at public universities, and their graduates earn about the same. If you want to be a civil engineer, choose an accredited program at the lowest cost you can, and plan for the FE exam and the PE license that will raise your pay.
