The most selective schools pay English graduates only a little more than the schools that admit most applicants, because the largest group of graduates goes into education, training and library jobs that mostly pay on set scales, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The cost runs the other way. A middle-income family typically pays about $27,000 over four years at schools that admit under a tenth of applicants, against about $65,000 at the open schools most students attend.

At an ordinary school, the debt you leave with matters more than the name. Two public programs 14 miles apart in the New York area pay about the same, yet one leaves graduates owing about $9,500 and the other about $27,000, or 4.2 months of pay against 12.1. Nearly half of English graduates go on to earn an advanced degree, more than across all fields, the BLS reports, so low debt leaves room for a teaching credential, law school or other graduate study.
