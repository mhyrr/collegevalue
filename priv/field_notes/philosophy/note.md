Philosophy graduates often start in law school or graduate school. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that 59 percent of philosophy and religion degree holders have an advanced degree, against 38 percent across all fields, and lawyers are among their most common jobs. Their first-year pay shows where they start, and many are still on their way to a law degree or a graduate program.

Graduate school brings its own bills, so plan to finish your bachelor's owing as little as you can. At schools that admit most applicants, a middle-income family typically pays about $59,000 over four years, and graduates' loans take 9.7 months of first-year pay. Apply to some selective schools too, where that same family typically pays about $25,000, and compare every offer on net price before you choose.
