Fine Arts graduates of schools that admit under a tenth of applicants earn barely more in their first year than graduates of schools that admit most who apply. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that most fine artists are self-employed and sell their own work. No employer pays extra for a famous school's name.

Cost is the choice that changes the outcome. Two programs 10 miles apart in the San Francisco Bay Area pay about the same, yet one leaves graduates owing 5.8 months of first-year pay and the other 17.1 months. For-profit schools typically cost a middle-income family about $147,000 over four years, against about $57,000 at public universities, and their graduates earn less.
