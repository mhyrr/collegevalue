Student debt barely moves with the school in Computer Engineering: at public, private and for-profit programs alike, the typical loan takes 4.1 to 4.5 months of first-year pay to repay. The real difference is the family's bill. Four years at a for-profit costs a middle-income family about $128,000, or 2.8 years of first-year pay, and its graduates earn the least of any kind of school.

Most of those for-profit graduates come from DeVry's engineering technology degree, which DeVry offers online and hybrid for working adults. Private nonprofits cost a middle-income family about $90,000 and pay only a little more than public universities. If you want to be a computer engineer, start at a public university, where a middle-income family typically pays about $56,000 for four years.
