Most of this major's graduates come from programs built for people already working in fire service, so the pay figures largely reflect firefighters finishing a degree. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says departments hire with a high school diploma, a few months at a fire academy and usually EMT certification, and the bachelor's mostly helps firefighters move up into officer and administrative jobs. Southern Illinois University Carbondale's top figure comes from a track that admits only students with public safety training, credentials or work experience.

A student starting from scratch at a school that admits most applicants should expect closer to about $46,000 in the first year, with the typical loan taking six months of that pay. Public programs typically cost a middle-income family about $46,000 over four years. The two private nonprofit programs cost about $112,000 and pay less.
