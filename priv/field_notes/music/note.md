The three best-paid Music programs are all in Texas, where many Bachelor of Music degrees include state teacher certification, according to the schools' own degree pages. Those graduates start as school music teachers on district pay scales. Selectivity barely matters: graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn about as much as those from schools that admit under a tenth.

A famous name won't raise your first paycheck, so cost decides the deal. Public programs typically cost a middle-income family about $60,000 over four years, while private nonprofits run about $95,000, and their graduates earn less. If you plan to perform, expect a master's after this degree, as the Rochester Business Journal found most conservatory seniors at one top school do; if you want a steady salary, choose a public program that includes teacher certification.
