At for-profit colleges, the typical Marketing graduate owes 10.8 months of first-year pay, against 6.7 months at public universities, though for-profit graduates earn about the same. Two programs 15 miles apart in the New York area show the gap. Graduates of the public one owe 5.2 months of pay, and graduates of the for-profit one owe 13.4 months while earning a little less.

Marketing leads straight to entry jobs such as marketing assistant, with no license or required next degree, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That makes the debt you leave with the number to compare. At schools that admit most applicants, a middle-income family typically pays about $68,000 over four years, and federal loans cover only part of it, so check each school's net price before you choose.
