Math pays in two markets. Graduates of schools that admit under a tenth of applicants earn about $70,000 their first year, and a middle-income family pays about $25,000 over four years for those programs. At the schools that admit most applicants, graduates earn about $41,500 and the same family pays about $64,000.

First-year pay also understates where many math majors end up: the Bureau of Labor Statistics finds that half hold an advanced degree, and their jobs run from software developer and data scientist to schoolteacher. At a typical school, a public program leaves graduates owing about 6.3 months of first-year pay. If you study math there, keep the cost low and choose early between teaching, data work and graduate school, so you take the classes and internships that path needs.
