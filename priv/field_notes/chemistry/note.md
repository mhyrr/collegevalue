Chemistry graduates earn about the same in their first year whether their school admits under a tenth of applicants or most of them; an American Chemical Society survey found new chemists' starting pay closely bunched, with lab and quality-control work the usual first job everywhere. Cost runs the other way. At schools that admit most applicants, a middle-income family typically pays about $63,000 over four years, against about $37,000 at the most selective schools.

The same survey found about a third of new chemistry graduates went straight on to Ph.D., medical or pharmacy programs, so first-year pay leaves many of them out. If you plan to study Chemistry, choose the school on what your family will actually pay, and apply to a few selective schools too, since their aid can make them the cheaper option. Keep your debt low if you expect more school after the bachelor's.
