In Teacher Education, how selective a college is barely changes a graduate's first paycheck. Graduates of schools that admit most applicants earn the same median as graduates of schools that admit a quarter to three fifths of applicants. Federal school staffing surveys show why: nearly every public school district pays teachers on a fixed salary schedule set by degree and years of experience, so the district that hires a new teacher sets the starting pay.

That makes cost the choice that counts. Public and private nonprofit programs pay about the same, yet a middle-income family typically pays about $55,000 over four years at a public school and about $83,000 at a private one. The Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that public school teachers need a state certification or license on top of the degree, so check that a program leads to a license in the state where you plan to teach.
